# CHALK — game lore (canonical)

## The setting

- **Year:** 2024
- **Place:** Montreal — Old Saint-Laurent district
- **Real-world anchor:** October 17, 2018 — Canada legalizes marijuana. Real event, fictional consequence: social-attitude shift opens the door for what comes next.
- **Tone:** quiet opening (yellow school buses, church bells, the hum of Decarie Boulevard) → societal collapse via body-horror infection
- **Mood:** modern urban survival horror. Not sci-fi, not post-apocalyptic-wasteland. Recent, recognizable, civilian.

## The drug

- **Official name:** Elysium — distributed via glossy government brochures under "harm reduction" programs
- **Street name:** Chalk
  - "Chalk" because the drug looks like compressed off-white powder blocks that crumble to dust
  - "Chalk" because addicts' skin loses color, turns gray/dry/rough — as if dusted with crushed chalk
- **Behavior model:** acts like Tranq (xylazine) — but mutated
  - Phase 1: users "freeze" in unnatural poses, feel nothing while body rots alive (ulcers, severe necrosis)
  - Phase 2: drug burns away humanity — leaves primal, animalistic aggression
- **Result:** rotting, chalk-white-skinned creatures driven by one thirst — to kill

## The hook (game antagonist)

- "And that was exactly what *they* were waiting for."
- An unnamed faction engineered/exploited the legalization gap to distribute Elysium under cover of government harm-reduction programs
- Reveal/scope of "they" is a story beat — design space for late-game reveal, faction layer, conspiracy ladder

## Anchor locations (referenced in opening)

- **Old Saint-Laurent** — primary game district
- **Du Collège metro station** — addicts gather here in the dark alleys nearby (early-game environmental storytelling beat)
- **Decarie Boulevard** — the familiar hum, normality before collapse (opening cinematic beat)

## Game-design implications (what the lore commits us to)

These are *systemic* commitments that flow from the lore. Things that would feel wrong to the lore should not be added; things that the lore implies should be planned for.

### Enemies
- Not "zombies" — **infected drug addicts.** Mid-stage humanity-loss creatures with rotting flesh, chalk-gray skin, animalistic movement.
- Phase variety implied: frozen statues (Phase 1, dangerous but immobile until disturbed) vs aggressive infected (Phase 2). Enables stealth-vs-combat encounter design.
- Body horror is canonical — ulcers, necrosis. Visual design and damage VFX should lean into rotting flesh, not generic undead.
- AI behavior: primal, aggressive — fits BT designs with no negotiation states (no dialogue, no surrender). Pure threat archetype.

### Environment / art direction
- Contemporary Montreal — recognizable architecture, yellow school buses, church spires, French/English bilingual signage, brutalist 70s metro stations, Decarie expressway aesthetic.
- Time of day shift implied: the opening is bright (school buses, daytime hum) — the collapse contrasts. Day-to-night arc is a tonal lever.
- Real Montreal landmarks should be researched as visual references (Mile End / Plateau / Saint-Laurent strip / Du Collège metro).
- Hazardous environmental storytelling: needles, government brochures, dust piles, frozen poses in alleyways.

### Player gear / inventory
- Civilian-modern weapons. **No sci-fi.** Hunting rifles, sidearms, improvised melee, civilian firearms. Likely some police/SQ (Sûreté du Québec) gear midgame.
- Body-protection gear makes lore-sense (gas masks against Chalk dust inhalation, hazmat for ulcer/blood contact) — gates encounter design.
- The existing inventory + weapon systems in AZ map cleanly to this — no changes needed mechanically. Tone/asset choices should honor it.

### Factions (implied but unconfirmed)
- **The infected** — primary threat
- **"They" / Elysium architects** — hidden faction, late-reveal candidate
- Implied secondary factions worth designing space for:
  - Government / corrupt institutional layer (the ones who pushed "harm reduction")
  - Civilian survivors / militia / resistance
  - Criminal networks profiting from distribution
- **Open question:** is the player a civilian, a cop/SQ, a recovering addict, or someone tied to the conspiracy? Each is a different game.

### Narrative pacing
- Cold open in normalcy (the school bus / church bells / Decarie passage) is a strong intro hook — should be the literal first 5 minutes
- The "It all started quietly" opening is a player-facing narrative line — could be used verbatim
- The "they were waiting" reveal is the dramatic spine — should not be exposited early

### Tone do's and don'ts
- **Do:** body horror, urban decay, recognizable real-world spaces gone wrong, drug as a slow rot
- **Do:** bilingual French/English flavor (signage, voice lines, ambient radio)
- **Don't:** generic zombie tropes (no brain-eating, no shambling-horde-only behavior)
- **Don't:** military-shooter clichés (no shadow government black ops as the player's team)
- **Don't:** sci-fi escalation (no zombie virus alien origin)

## Open / deferred design questions

- Who is the player? (Civilian / cop / recovering addict / undercover journalist / member of "they")
- Game scope — single neighborhood (Old Saint-Laurent) deep, or expanding map to broader Montreal?
- Drug as mechanic — does the player risk infection? Phase 1 freeze vulnerability if exposed? Antidote system?
- Multiplayer / co-op fit — supported by the lore (survivor groups make sense) but not committed
- Time scope of campaign — first days of outbreak, weeks after, or a year-later "remnant" setting?
- Endgame — survival, escape Montreal, expose the conspiracy, find a cure, all of the above?
